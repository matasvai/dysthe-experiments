Dysthe curves start from the identical projected initial profile within each family. These short-time examples
exhibit peak growth in the nonlinear models rather than decay. No decay exponent is assumed.

These packet examples illustrate evolution and spectral redistribution; they do not by themselves
demonstrate Benjamin–Feir instability. That test requires a weakly perturbed wavetrain and
measurement of sideband growth against a specified linearized prediction.

Figure 7: A separate initial-bandwidth control through T = 0.1. The first two panels use cutoff 24. The
lower left compares the initial cutoffs with the analytic periodized Gaussian; the lower right uses the same
fixed tail region throughout time. A logarithmic scale magnifies very small differences; zero differences are
below its display floor.

Projecting the Gaussian through mode 12 creates a maximum initial discrepancy of approximately
3.77 × 10−9 . Projection through mode 24 reduces that discrepancy to floating-point roundoff.
Therefore, features at the first scale cannot be attributed solely to nonlinear dynamics without this
input check. The short high-cutoff control also passes a finer time-step comparison.
The main envelope changes little on this near-zero interval. A magnified plot must retain its scale
label: tiny oscillations in the tails can look dramatic even while the main amplitude remains almost
unchanged. The original level-surface rule, 30% of each current peak, would hide these features
entirely.

Here |Ds | has Fourier symbol |k|. This is the longitudinal temporal potential-envelope restriction,
not the approved spatial free-surface model and not the historical three-coordinate canonical linear
equation. The coefficient convention is labelled stability-consistent; the literal printed 1979 equation
has the previously documented discrepancy. The source is K. B. Dysthe, Proc. R. Soc. Lond. A
369 (1979), 105–114, equations (2.19) and (3.2)–(3.3). This visual exercise makes no new choice of
physical setting.
The coordinates are A = ϵu, s = ϵ(x − t/2) and T = ϵ2 t. A physical carrier and potential-tosurface conversion are needed before displaying calibrated water elevation. The extra carrier in the

Thus it has no numerical time-step error. The NLS control suppresses all terms multiplied by ϵ; it

Checks. Nonlinear calculations use complex128 Fourier differentiation, cubic dealiasing and
integrating-factor RK4 at N = 1024, h = 0.0025. Independent comparisons use N = 512 at the
same step and h = 0.005 at the same grid. The maximum sampled relative errors must satisfy
E0 ≤ 10−5 , E1 ≤ 10−4 and E2 ≤ 10−3 ; action drift and retained-edge action fraction must be at
most 10−6 and 10−8 . All six nonlinear model–input runs pass. All three linear controls preserve
mode power to numerical precision. There are 117 saved times, including increments of 0.005 near
zero.
Inputs. The Gaussian has amplitude 0.85, width 3, centre −2 and chirp 0.08. The random
profile is a saved smooth complex Fourier polynomial. The new pair comprises two amplitude-0.6
Gaussians of width 2.2, centred at ±5 with relative phase π/3. The main comparisons use initial
cutoff 12; a separate Gaussian audit compares cutoffs 12 and 24. The scripts and JSON metadata
define the complete profiles.
Reproduction. The source bundle contains separate scripts for reference evolution, initialtail auditing and plotting. Run them in the order listed in the README. The PDF source is
report/main.tex; animation generation requires FFmpeg. Full complex arrays, numerical checks
and the unchanged solver snapshot are included. No PINN or cluster training is involved in this
visual companion.
Interpretation. These are finite-time periodic computations, not proof of global existence, a
physical breaking prediction or an asymptotic decay result. The next nonlinear-instability panel
should compare seeded sideband growth in physical space and Fourier space; NOAA remains a later,
separately calibrated comparison.

Nonlinear targets. The new CPU framework targets 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024 in two
tracks: periodic evolution/recurrence, and localized-packet calculations with independent domain
enlargement. A failed refinement or domain check blocks later stages. The small smoke calculations
verify execution only; nonlinear T = 1024 has not been run or qualified here. At ϵ = 0.05, T = 1024
corresponds to dimensionless carrier time 409600, so it is a demanding extension of the current
short-time runs.
Use the supplied long_horizon/README.md for configuration and CPU cluster commands. Domain, grid and step may need to grow substantially; an estimate from a short benchmark cannot
include those unknown costs. Training a PINN is not required for either numerical track.
